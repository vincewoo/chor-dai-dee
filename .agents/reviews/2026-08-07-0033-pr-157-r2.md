## What the two commits do

`33c08b5` replaces the name-keyed `roundHistoryByName` object with a
seat-indexed `roundHistoryBySeat` **array**. `recordDealStrength()` pushes at
`roundHistoryBySeat[seat]`; `updateScores()` resolves `this.seatOf(s.id)` and
returns early on `-1`; `describeRoundReview()` now iterates `this.players` and
emits one entry per seat keyed by that seat's *current* name. The `delete
entry.mean` moved out of the ranking loop into a second pass. Five tests were
added and the two false `dragon_win` comments were rewritten.

`7bb6578` changes `describeRoundReview`'s accumulator to `Object.create(null)`
and adds a test that round-trips the payload through `JSON.parse(JSON.stringify(…))`.

## Findings

### Major

---

**M1 — `describeRoundReview()` resolves names ~135 lines and two `await`s after
the standings resolved theirs, so a `leave_room` landing in that window puts the
review keys and the standings names out of sync — and r1's C2 symptom
("nobody is 1st") comes straight back.**
`server/index.js:1055-1059` vs `:1191`

`handleRoundOver` builds `scoresWithCumulative` from `calculateRoundScores(…,
room.players)` at line 1059 — capturing each seat's name **then**. It then
awaits `withTransaction(…)` (line 1092) and `gamelogRecorder.recordRoundEnd(…)`
(line 1147) before calling `room.describeRoundReview()` at line 1191, which reads
`player.name` **now**. `room.gameState` is not set to `'finished'` until line
1169, so throughout both awaits the `leave_room` handler still takes its
mid-game branch and calls `replaceWithBot()`, renaming a live seat to
`Bot (Alice)`.

Reproduced against the real `Room`, replaying that exact call order:

```
scores names   (built at :1059): [ 'Alice', 'Bot 2', 'Bot 3', 'Bot 4' ]
roundReview keys (built at :1191): [ 'Bot (Alice)', 'Bot 2', 'Bot 3', 'Bot 4' ]

standings row "Alice" -> dealRank = NULL   (falls back to "Nth place")
standings row "Bot 2" -> dealRank = 4
standings row "Bot 3" -> dealRank = 2
standings row "Bot 4" -> dealRank = 3
```

Three rows render `2nd`, `3rd`, `4th`; **no row is 1st**, because rank 1 went to
a key with no standings row — which is precisely the failure r1's C2 described
and this commit set out to remove. The affected player's row also silently drops
to the `Nth place` fallback while the panel keeps `Bot (Alice)`'s round columns
in the union, so their cells render blank.

This is a **regression window opened by the seat keying**: under the old
name-keying the key was frozen at deal time (`Alice`) and therefore matched the
standings name that was also captured before the awaits. Seat keying resolves the
name late, so it is now the only field in the payload that can move after the
rest of the payload was built.

Probability is low — the window is one SQLite transaction plus a gamelog write —
but the trigger is a player hitting Leave at the exact moment the game ends,
which is when players leave. The dragon path is **not** affected: I checked
`handleDragonWin` (`index.js:838-878`) and there is no `await` between
`calculateDragonScores` and `describeRoundReview()`.

*Fix (cheapest first):* call `room.describeRoundReview()` immediately after
`room.updateScores(roundScores)` and hold it, so it is captured in the same tick
as `scoresWithCumulative`. Alternatively key the emitted object on `s.id`
(the standings rows carry ids too) rather than on a name resolved at a different
instant, which removes the coupling entirely.

---

**M2 — A mid-game joiner is now credited with the deals of whoever held the seat
before them, including the game-level `dealRank`.**
`server/game/RoomManager.js:1037-1055`, docblock at `:1019-1026`

`replaceBot()` overwrites the seat in place, so the newcomer inherits the whole
seat history. Reproduced with the real `replaceBot()`:

```
before join:  Alice / Bot 2 / Bot 3 / Bot 4, two rounds played
Charlie joins at round 3, taking Bot 2's seat

  Alice    rank=4 avg=39 rounds=[1,2,3]
  Charlie  rank=1 avg=64 rounds=[1,2,3]   <- rounds 1 and 2 were dealt to Bot 2
  Bot 3    rank=3 avg=40 rounds=[1,2,3]
  Bot 4    rank=2 avg=52 rounds=[1,2,3]

Charlie.joinedAtRound = 2      (present on the player object, unused)
Charlie.joinedWithScore = 78
```

Charlie's standings row reads **`Deal strength: 1st (64th percentile)`** — the
loudest statement the screen makes about luck — computed from thirteen cards he
never saw. The drill-in panel then prints R1 and R2 cells for him, with their
tier labels and percentiles, under his name.

The docblock argues this is deliberate ("one continuous history under the
replacement's name, matching `cumulativeScores`"), and the score half of it does
line up (`totalPoints = 117 === cumulativeScores['c1']`). But an inherited
*score* is disclosed — `replaceBot` sets `joinedWithScore` and the server emits
`mid_game_join_info` telling the joiner exactly that — whereas an inherited
*deal ranking* is presented as a fact about the player, and the panel's `won`
cells also credit them with rounds a bot won. Note also that
`describeParticipants()` (`RoomManager.js:2277`) resolves the **opposite**
direction for the DB write, attributing a botified seat to `replacedHuman`, so
the two consumers of the same seat now disagree about who a seat belongs to.

The bot case is defensible on its own — a row literally named `Bot (Alice)`
showing Alice's rounds reads correctly, and a returning player recovers their
own seat (see "Verified safe"). It is specifically the human-joiner case that
mis-attributes.

Compounding it, **the docblock two sentences up says the opposite**:

> A seat that missed rounds -- someone who joined mid-game, or a round in flight
> when the server restarted -- simply has fewer, which is why the mean is
> computed over the entries present rather than over roundNumber.

Under seat keying a mid-game joiner does **not** have fewer rounds; they have
exactly as many as everyone else. That sentence is the one a future reader would
use to conclude this finding cannot happen.

*Fix:* either annotate/limit the entry using `joinedAtRound` (already on the
player), or slice the seat's history at the join for a human replacement while
keeping it whole for a bot one, or accept it and correct the docblock — but
`dealRank` in particular should not be computed from rounds the row's occupant
did not play.

### Moderate

---

**S1 — `roundHistoryBySeat` is positional, but `this.players` can shrink and
reorder, and nothing clears the array when it does.**
`server/game/RoomManager.js:833` (the only reset)

The array is allocated once per game inside `startGame()`'s `roundNumber === 0`
branch. Five paths mutate `this.players` by filter, and none of them touch it:
`removePlayer` (`:755`), `kickPlayer`'s non-playing branch (`:469`),
`removePlayerPostGame` (`:2477`), `transitionToLobby` (`:2356`) and
`startRematch` (`:2421`).

Reproduced — a kick from the post-game screen shifts every seat below it:

```
before kick:  Alice=61  Bob=88  Bot 3=32  Bot 4=42
kick Bob (gameState 'finished')
after  kick:  Alice=61  Bot 3=88  Bot 4=32
              ^ Bot 3 is now reporting Bob's deal, Bot 4 is reporting Bot 3's
```

This is **latent, not live**: `describeRoundReview()` is called exactly twice,
both at game over, and the result is cached into `room.lastGameResults`; every
replay path re-emits the cached object rather than recomputing. So no client sees
the shifted view today. But the safety rests entirely on "nobody calls this
method again", which is not what the code says anywhere, and a positional array
that outlives the roster it indexes is a trap for the next person who wants a
review on `round_over` or in `getGameState()`.

*Fix:* clear `roundHistoryBySeat` in `transitionToLobby()` and after any roster
filter, or initialise it in the constructor next to `roundsWonByName`
(`:88`) and null it whenever `players` is rebuilt.

---

**S2 — The un-reset `start_game` path (r1 Q5) is strictly worse under seat
keying: game 2 shows game 1's rounds attributed to different players.**
`server/index.js:1794` + `RoomManager.js:811`

`start_game` has no `gameState` guard, so calling it while a room is `'finished'`
skips the `roundNumber === 0` reset. Combined with `removePlayerPostGame` (which
shrinks `players`), reproduced:

```
game 1: Alice / Bob / Bot 3 / Bot 4, 2 rounds
Alice leaves the post-game screen  -> seats = [ Bob, Bot 3, Bot 4 ]
start_game again (roundNumber = 2, so no reset)

seats   = [ 'Bob', 'Bot 3', 'Bot 4', 'Bot 4' ]     <- duplicate bot name
history = [ 3, 3, 3, 3 ]                            <- game 1's rounds retained
review  = Bob rounds=[1,2,3]   (rounds 1-2 were Alice's)
          rows = 3 for 4 seats
```

Two things fall out. Bob's review carries Alice's deals; and the auto-fill's
`Bot ${this.players.length + 1}` naming produced **two seats named `Bot 4`**,
which collapses one review row (3 rows, ranks 1..3, for a four-seat table) —
falsifying the `dealRank` "1..4 with no gaps" invariant the commit's own comment
and new test assert.

Both the missing guard and the bot-naming collision are pre-existing and out of
this PR's scope. Flagged because this PR is the first thing that renders the
consequences, and because a one-line `if (room.gameState !== 'waiting') return;`
on `start_game` closes all of it.

**S3 — The new tests hand-fake the two swaps instead of calling
`replaceWithBot()` / `replaceBot()`, which is exactly what r1's C4 asked for.**
`server/test/dealStrength.test.js`

```js
// 'deal strength ranks exactly the seats on the game-over screen'
room.players[1] = { id: 'c1', name: 'Charlie', isBot: false, hand: [], joinedMidGame: true };

// 'a seat keeps one history when its player walks out mid-game'
room.players[0].name = 'Bot (Alice)';
room.players[0].isBot = true;
```

Neither function under discussion is ever invoked. The tests pin the *shape* the
author assumed those functions produce, so they cannot catch a change to the
functions themselves — if `replaceWithBot` ever spliced rather than assigned in
place, or if `replaceBot` stopped taking the first bot's index, both tests would
still pass while the feature broke. Calling the real `replaceBot()` would also
have surfaced M2 as an explicit assertion (`rounds.length === 3` for a player
who joined at round 3) rather than leaving it unnoticed.

Two smaller gaps in the same file:

- the tie test asserts only that two seats *share* a rank; it does not pin what
  the rank after a tie is. Verified behaviour is competition ranking (`1,1,3,4`
  and `1,1,1,4`), which is the other half of "matches `rankTable`".
- `assert.ok(!(departed in review))` is a weak check — `in` on a null-prototype
  object is fine here, but the assertion passes for any reason the name is
  absent, including the seat vanishing entirely.

**S4 — The replacement `dragon_win` comment states one number where the real
distribution is bimodal, and nearly half of dragons score *below average*.**
`server/index.js:871-876`

> rawDealStrength prices control against plays needed to shed, and thirteen
> distinct ranks is thirteen plays: it scores 2 -- "Strong", 69th percentile

Over 20,000 random-suit dragons scored with the repo's own `DealStrength`:

| outcome | share |
|---|---|
| `Strong`, raw 2, pct 69 | 40.8% |
| `Strong`, raw 3, pct 76.5 | 13.4% |
| `Below average`, raw −2, pct 32 | 34.6% |
| `Below average`, raw −1, pct 42 | 11.1% |

**45.5% of dragons are "Below average".** The driver is `playsNeeded`, which is
greedy: depending on the suits it finds two five-card combinations (5 plays) or
none (9 plays) — never the "thirteen plays" the comment asserts, since a dragon
holds every rank and therefore several straights. The comment's *conclusion* is
right and in fact understated; only its specific figure is the optimistic half.

The accompanying test pins `SUITS[i % 4]`, one arbitrary suit assignment that
lands in the 54.5% Strong half, so `assert.strictEqual(d.tierKey, 'strong')`
does not test the general claim — a differently-suited dragon asserts `weak`.

*Fix:* state the range ("Strong or Below average depending on suits, 32nd to
76th percentile"), and either assert `tierKey !== 'premium'` or sample several
suit assignments.

**S5 — `roundsWonByName` is the same prototype-name hazard `7bb6578` fixed, one
function away, and its value reaches the database.**
`server/game/RoomManager.js:1582`, `:2285` → `server/index.js:318-328`

`7bb6578` hardened `describeRoundReview`'s accumulator but left the sibling
accumulator the same commit's comment block sits above. Verified for a player
registered as `constructor` / `toString` (both accepted by `validateUsername`):

```
roundsWonByName['constructor']        -> function   (Object)
describeParticipants()[0].roundsWon   -> function Object() { [native code] }
```

That value is passed straight through as `roundsWon:` to `saveGameParticipant()`
on the abandoned-game path (`index.js:327`). `__proto__` gives `{}` instead. Both
are pre-existing (r1 noted the pattern degrades silently rather than throwing),
but the commit title — "keep prototype-named players on the scoreboard" — reads
as if the class is closed, and the fix is the same one-word change.

Worth reconsidering r1's closing suggestion that `username.js` reject
`Object.prototype` key names outright, given how many accumulators in this file
are keyed on a username.

### Verified safe

Each of these was executed, not reasoned about.

- **The tie fix works.** Moving `delete` to a second pass makes the branch fire:
  forced ties give `Alice=1 Bot 2=1 Bot 3=3 Bot 4=3`, and a three-way chain gives
  `1,1,1,4`. No `mean` survives on any entry. There is also no floating-point
  hazard: `percentileFor` returns half-integers only, which are exact in binary,
  so two seats with the same multiset of percentiles produce bit-identical means
  regardless of summation order.
- **`Object.create(null)` survives the wire, end to end.** Verified with the real
  `socket.io-parser` from `server/node_modules`, not just `JSON`:
  `2["game_over",{"roundReview":{"__proto__":{…},"Alice":{…}}}]` decodes to
  `Object.keys → ['__proto__','Alice']`, `hasOwnProperty('__proto__') === true`,
  the looked-up value is the entry, and `Object.getPrototypeOf(decoded)` is a
  clean `Object.prototype`. The same holds for `constructor`, `toString`,
  `hasOwnProperty` and `valueOf`.
- **The client's lookups degrade safely for an absent name.** On the parsed plain
  object, `roundReview['constructor']` returns the `Object` function, but
  `?.avgPercentile ?? null` → `null` and `entry?.rounds || []` → `[]`, so
  `GameOverV2` falls back to "Nth place" and `RoundReviewPanel` renders an empty
  row. No crash, no wrong number.
- **`updateScores`'s `if (seat < 0) return;` is unreachable and drops nothing.**
  Both callers build `roundScores` from `room.players` (`index.js:841` and
  `:1055`) and call `updateScores` on the very next statement with no `await`
  between, so every `s.id` resolves. It is pure defence.
- **Reconnect.** `reconnectPlayer()` changes `id` only — seat index and name are
  untouched — so the review is unaffected, and the entry still closes out
  correctly after the id change. Verified.
- **Rematch and lobby restart.** Both set `roundNumber = 0`, so `startGame()`
  allocates a fresh `[]`. Verified including a lobby restart that adds a *new*
  human before restarting: history comes back `[1,1,1,1]` against the new roster.
- **Walk out, then come back.** `replaceBot` takes the first bot index, which is
  the walked-out seat, so a returning player lands back on their own seat and
  gets one continuous history — strictly better than the split r1 described.
  Verified.
- **Duplicate names would collapse a row** (verified: 3 rows, ranks 1..3, for a
  four-seat table) — but `index.js:602` rejects a duplicate human name at join,
  and auto-fill bot names are unique within a fill, so it is unreachable outside
  S2's un-reset restart. Guest names (`Guest_1234`, 9,000 values, generated
  client-side with no uniqueness check) collide only across rooms, where it does
  not matter.
- **Spectators** live in a separate `Map` (`RoomManager.js:130`) and never enter
  `players`, so they cannot shift a seat index.
- **`this.players` empty** returns an empty null-prototype object; no throw. A
  `players` array *longer* than the history is handled by `?.[seat] || []`.

### Nits

- `recordDealStrength`'s `this.roundHistoryBySeat ||= []` (`:1088`) is still the
  defensive guard r1 flagged, and the constructor still does not initialise the
  field while `roundsWonByName` is initialised at `:88`. The array now has a
  second reason to be constructor-initialised (S1).
- `if (!rounds.length) return;` (`:1040`) is now genuinely reachable — a seat
  with no deal is dropped from `out`, giving `rows < seats` and ranks 1..3.
  Verified by forcing it. Emitting the entry with `dealRank: null` instead of
  omitting the row would keep the client's format uniform (r1 S6's mixed-format
  point) and make the "no gaps" invariant true by construction.
- The seat-keying comment (`:823-832`) leads with "Socket ids change on
  reconnect" as the reason names were rejected. Ids were never the shipped
  alternative; renames were the whole problem. The paragraph is accurate but
  buries its own point.
- The comment at `:1063-1066` — "Only seats are in `out` at all now, so this
  ranks exactly the rows on screen: 1..4 with no gaps" — is false in the two
  cases above (a zero-round seat, and duplicate names). Both are unreachable
  today; the claim is stronger than the code.
- `describeRoundReview()` returns the live `rounds` arrays by reference, and
  `updateScores` still mutates them post-hoc. Unchanged from r1's assessment and
  still safe (the reset replaces the container), but M1 is a reminder that this
  method is not a snapshot.

## Recommendation

`33c08b5` and `7bb6578` genuinely close r1's C1, C2, C3, C3b and C6, and I could
not find a swap sequence that reproduces any of them in the steady state. The two
things I would not merge without are **M1** (a two-line move that closes a
window reintroducing the exact bug the commit fixes) and a decision on **M2** —
either the behaviour changes or the docblock stops claiming the opposite.
S1 and S3 are cheap and would make the invariant real rather than assumed.
